# A sceptic's read of the arguments

Written 13 Nov 2026. Two careful readers go through the seven experiments and the eight
"Not convinced?" replies in the verdict, as they appear in the live build. No app copy has been
changed. The proposed rewrites at the end wait for the owner's approval.

**The compatibilist** thinks free will is real and compatible with determinism. Freedom means
acting for your own reasons, being responsive to reasons, and not being coerced. The self is
the whole reasoning system, not a little watcher behind the eyes. (Think Dennett.) SPEC §2
names this person as the primary reader: "may be a compatibilist without the label".

**The libertarian** thinks free will is real and needs the ability to do otherwise with
everything else the same. Either the agent itself is a cause (agent causation), or indeterminism
inside a genuine effort of will leaves the outcome up to the agent (Kane). The lay version says
the soul does the choosing.

Marks: **lands** (the reader concedes the point), **wobbles** (they push back but stay),
**bounces** (they stop trusting the app here).

## The top three weaknesses

1. **The compatibilist gets the thinnest answer.** The reply written for them ("Free will just
   means not being forced") is fifth of eight, six screens long, and ends on "keeps the name and
   changes the subject", which reads as a dismissal. The app's recurring move, "you didn't
   choose X", invites an obvious comeback that it never meets: no possible being could choose
   its own starting point, so lacking that is no discovery. The one place Harris has a real
   answer to that is desert (blame, resentment, pride), and the app keeps desert in a different
   reply.
2. **The libertarian's "third thing" is never named.** The libertarian answers "Yes, I could
   have" on Rewind the tape and is told, in eight words, "Whatever tipped the balance wasn't you.
   It was chance." The dice-roll reply and Swap places ("nothing of you left over") assume the
   very thing the libertarian denies. Harris's own answer is missing. Run the dilemma on the agent
   or the soul itself: why did it tip this way? Either something made it, or nothing did.
3. **A few lines overreach, and a careful reader catches them.** Heartbeat and dreams ("I am my
   brain"), "you would never break a resolution" (veto), "the tumour is simply a cause we can
   see" (responsibility), "most people find no one there" (feeling of choosing). Each is one
   screen, but one weak line makes a sceptic discount the strong ones around it.

## The experiments

**Pick a city** (step A, walkthrough 1)
- *Compatibilist.* Happy to agree. A no-stakes pick is the least free-will-like case there is, and
  nobody locates freedom in it. "You can't choose what doesn't come to mind" is true and harmless,
  since options are always bounded. As the first experiment it risks a "so what?" The "That was
  trivial" chip, which answers exactly this, only appears on The urge.
- *Libertarian.* Agrees the options arrived and puts freedom in the selecting. "Did the reason come
  first?" is a good screen. It plants the idea of after-the-fact reasons with no study needed.

**The next thought** (A, walkthrough 2)
- *Compatibilist.* Waiting for a stray thought is the least directed thing a mind does. Real
  deliberation sets a problem, and relevant thoughts arrive because of your goals. The name line,
  "every decision you make is built out of thoughts like these", is the leap where they get off.
- *Libertarian.* Concedes thoughts arise, and says choosing is something the agent does with them.
- *Strong bit.* "The intention to do that would itself be a thought" closes the obvious cheat.

**The urge** (A, library only)
- *Compatibilist.* Same arbitrariness worry as the city. The chip reply "More steps don't add an
  author" is where they push. To them, the extra steps (weighing, revising, endorsing) are what
  authorship consists of.
- *Libertarian.* "That would have taken an earlier urge to do the summoning" is a clean regress and
  lands. The natural comeback is the veto, which the verdict covers.

**Ask why** (B, library only)
- *Both readers.* Agree that reasons bottom out in preferences nobody chose. No compatibilist ever
  needed chosen foundations. The name line ("Your reasons were real, and they did real work") is
  the best-calibrated line in the app. It concedes what's true before pressing.
- *Small mismatch.* It sits under "Could you have done otherwise?" in the library, but it argues
  step C (where wants come from).

**Rewind the tape** (B, walkthrough 3). The core of the app.
- *Libertarian.* Home ground, and they answer Yes. They are told "Whatever tipped the balance
  wasn't you. It was chance." An agent-causal libertarian says "I tipped it". Kane says the agent
  was trying for both outcomes, so whichever won was willed. The app asserts chance where it could
  show it. Replay the decision a hundred times. If it goes one way sixty times and the other forty,
  with you identical on every run, what decided each run? That lets the reader watch chance at
  work.
- *Compatibilist.* The question is ambiguous to them. Asked "could you have chosen differently?",
  they may say Yes (meaning "I had the ability, and would have, had I wanted to") and get told it
  was chance. If they say No, they hear "you didn't choose the causes" and reply that nobody could,
  so it's no flaw in them.

**Choose what you want** (C, walkthrough 4)
- *Compatibilist.* Bounces. No one, compatibilist or libertarian, thinks you can switch off a desire
  by fiat. Frankfurt's version of free will is about which desires you act on and endorse.
  Producing desires on demand was never part of it. So the experiment refutes a view few people hold, and the
  name line ("you didn't choose what you want") gets "right, and freedom never needed that."
- *Libertarian.* Agrees desires are given and puts freedom in what you do with them.
- *Strong bit.* The Schopenhauer line lands with a lay reader. The "I can change what I want" chip
  handles self-improvement well.
- This is the walkthrough's only step C experiment, which is why the recommendation suggests
  trying Swap places in its slot.

**Swap places** (C, library only)
- *Compatibilist.* The strongest push in the app on this reader. It moves from "your desires" to
  "your whole make-up", then turns it back on them ("You didn't choose your genes, your parents or
  your experiences either"). They'll still say the capacities are real whoever got them, but they
  feel the weight.
- *Libertarian (dualist).* "Every part of them is in place. There is nothing of you left over."
  The lay libertarian says "my soul would be left over". The app assumes physicalism without
  saying so. Harris's answer (you didn't choose your soul either) is missing.

## The eight "Not convinced?" replies

**"I can feel myself choosing."** Raise a hand, then look for the chooser.
- *Libertarian.* Bounces on "Most people find no one there." It's a claim about other people the
  reader can't check, and many will say they did find themselves. A phenomenological libertarian
  adds that the agent is the one looking, and an eye can't see itself. The fix turns that move into
  a concession. If the chooser can't appear to itself, the feeling of choosing can't count as
  evidence for it either.
- *Compatibilist.* Agrees there's no little watcher, and says free will never needed one.
- Close to meditation instruction, which SPEC §3 put out of scope. Fine at this length.

**"I could have done otherwise."** "Yes, if you had wanted to. But could you have wanted to?"
- *Compatibilist.* Recognises the conditional analysis and then the regress. "You never chose how
  you were." Nor could anyone, so where's the loss? Wobbles, because the reply is clear and honest
  about the conditional reading.
- *Libertarian.* "Yes, I could have wanted to." The reply asserts "You didn't choose your mood"
  rather than showing it.

**"The universe isn't fully determined."** The dice roll in the brain.
- *Libertarian.* Bounces. No serious libertarian thinks a random roll settles decisions, so the dice
  is a caricature. "Mixing the two doesn't conjure up a third thing" is exactly where they'll say
  "the third thing is me." The reply should name that and run the dilemma on it.
- *Compatibilist.* Agrees with every word.

**"I am my brain, so I chose."**
- *Compatibilist.* This is their actual view, and the reply answers a straw version. Nobody
  identifies with their heartbeat. They identify with the reasons-weighing system, which is a
  distinctive part of the brain. "If that counts as you choosing, then you also choose your
  heartbeat" is the weakest line in the app for this reader. "That self is always the last to
  know" is a timing claim that leans on the Libet-style studies the spec keeps out, and
  introspection can't establish it.
- Better move. Grant the reasons-weighing self, then send it back to watch how reasons arrive and
  how much each one weighs. That joins up with the "But I deliberated" reply.

**"Free will just means not being forced."** The compatibilist's own objection, and where they'll
judge the whole app.
- *Strong bits.* It concedes that coercion, addiction and deception matter. The secretly implanted
  desires are the right weapon.
- *Where it bounces.* "Most people say that wouldn't be free will" is a claim about others, and the
  reader may be one of the people who'd call it free will. Compatibilists have worked replies here
  (history matters, or bite the bullet). "Keeps the name and changes the subject" sounds like a
  brush-off, because they think the subject people care about (control, responsibility, acting for
  reasons) is exactly what they kept. "It's not the freedom most people believe they have" is a
  contested claim about other people, and the spec bars citing studies, so it can't be backed.
- *Better move.* Concede outright that they have that freedom. Then test whether it's all they
  mean, through resentment and blame. That's the one place Harris and a compatibilist really
  disagree.

**"I can veto my impulses."**
- *Strong bit.* The veto is itself a thought that arises, and that lands with both readers.
- *Where it bounces.* "If you could, you would never break a resolution" doesn't follow. Having a
  capacity doesn't mean exercising it every time. Both readers catch this. Turn it into an
  experience instead. Recall a resolution you broke, and ask where the veto was then.

**"Then nothing matters."**
- Solid with both readers. The fatalism distinction is correct, and "the question *why bother?*
  arises on its own" is a nice turn.

**"Then no one is responsible."** The tumour case.
- *Compatibilist.* Bounces on "The tumour is simply a cause we can see." To them the tumour matters
  because it broke a capacity (impulse control). Being visible on a scan is beside the point. Responsibility
  tracks capacities, and most people have them. The reply needs to grant that, then make Harris's
  real point. Nobody chooses how much self-control they get, so a tumour is just one visible way to
  be unlucky.
- *Libertarian.* Reads the tumour case as an excusing condition that ordinary people lack. Same
  fix.
- Minor point. "every criminal's brain was shaped by causes he didn't choose" could be "they".

## Other screens worth a look

- **The verdict question.** "Has this convinced you that free will is an illusion?" A compatibilist's
  honest answer is "I agree with every observation, and I'd still call it free will." The yes/no
  pushes them to No, and the reply written for them is fifth on the list. A third answer, routed
  straight to that reply, would meet them where they are.
- **"Still not convinced" ending.** "Harris would say the evidence was never in the arguments
  anyway" reads as "you didn't look hard enough" to someone who just looked. Acknowledge the
  serious company they're in instead. The reading list already does this well.
- **The "yes" ending.** Seven screens in a spiritual register ("the universe is happening as you").
  It's for people who said yes, so the risk is low, but SPEC §3 asked for "one brief, calm note,
  not a sermon". Trimming to four or five screens would honour that.
- **Chip replies.** "I am my brain" ends "processes you neither see nor control". For a
  compatibilist, control means acting for reasons, so "nor control" assumes the conclusion.
  "Processes you never see" makes the point without that.

## Proposed rewrites (for approval)

Each new screen is 25 words or fewer. Nothing below has been applied.

**1. "Free will just means not being forced"** (`src/content/verdict.ts`, id `compatible`)
- Screen 5. Current: "Most people say that wouldn't be free will. Yet your genes and upbringing did the same thing,
  only more slowly."
  Proposed: "Would you call that free will? Your genes and upbringing did the same thing, only more
  slowly."
- Screen 6. Current: "Calling this free will keeps the name and changes the subject. It's not the freedom most
  people believe they have."
  Proposed, three screens:
  - "If freedom from force is all you mean by free will, you have it. Most people do."
  - "But is that all you mean? Think of someone you resent. Don't you feel they could have done
    better, just as they were?"
  - "That feeling asks for more than freedom from force. It asks for an author. That's the free
    will in question."

**2. "The universe isn't fully determined"** (id `random`)
- Last screen. Current: "Causes leave no room for you. Chance leaves none either. Mixing the two doesn't conjure up a
  third thing."
  Proposed, two screens:
  - "Perhaps you are the third thing. A self that tips the balance, neither caused nor random."
  - "Then why did it tip this way? Whatever answer you give is either a cause or no answer at all."

**3. Rewind the tape, "yes" branch** (`src/content/experiments.ts`, id `rewind`)
- Current: "Whatever tipped the balance wasn't you. It was chance."
  Proposed, two screens, before the existing coin-toss screen:
  - "Now replay it a hundred times. Suppose *“{decision}”* happens sixty times, and the other choice
    forty."
  - "You were identical on every run. So what decided each one? That is what we mean by chance."

**4. "I am my brain, so I chose"** (id `brain`)
- Screens 5 and 6. Current: "If that counts as you choosing, then you also choose your heartbeat, and tonight's dreams." /
  "That isn't the freedom people mean. They mean the conscious self as the author of its thoughts.
  And that self is always the last to know."
  Proposed:
  - "Perhaps you mean the part of your brain that weighs reasons and decides. That's a fair
    answer."
  - "So watch it weigh. Do you produce the reasons, or do they appear, already carrying their
    weight?"
  - Closing question: "When you weigh reasons, who decides how much each one weighs?"

**5. "I can veto my impulses"** (id `veto`)
- Last screen. Current: "If you could, you would never break a resolution. Anyone who has tried to give something up
  knows how that goes."
  Proposed: "Think of a resolution you broke. At the moment it broke, where was the veto? Did you
  choose its absence?"

**6. "Then no one is responsible"** (id `responsible`)
- Screen 4. Current: "Now consider that every criminal's brain was shaped by causes he didn't choose. The tumour is
  simply a cause we can see."
  Proposed, three screens:
  - "You might say the tumour matters because it broke his capacity for self-control. That's
    true."
  - "But no one chooses how much self-control they get. Genes and childhood hand it out
    unequally."
  - "A tumour is just one way to be unlucky. It happens to show up on a scan."

**7. "I can feel myself choosing"** (id `feel`)
- Last screen. Current: "Look closely for the chooser, the one who stands behind the choice and makes it. Most people
  find no one there."
  Proposed, two screens:
  - "Look closely for the chooser, the one who stands behind the choice and makes it."
  - "If you find nothing, you might say the chooser can't see itself. Then the feeling of choosing
    can't be evidence for it either."

**8. Swap places, "yes" branch** (id `swap`)
- New screen after "What would make the difference?…":
  "Perhaps you'd bring your soul along. But you didn't choose your soul, or what it tends to
  want."

**9. The next thought, name line** (id `next-thought`)
- Current: "You are not the author of your next thought. And every decision you make is built out of
  thoughts like these."
  Proposed: "You are not the author of your next thought. Even when you think on purpose, notice how
  each thought simply arrives."

**10. "Still not convinced" ending** (`UNCONVINCED`, screen 2)
- Current: "Harris would say the evidence was never in the arguments anyway. It's in your own experience,
  available whenever you look."
  Proposed: "You're in good company. Many careful philosophers think the freedom worth wanting survives
  everything you've just seen."
